## Slide 2

[View slide 2](README.md#slide-2)

```text
Ecosystem Functions
Major players in terrestrial ecosystems – abundant and highly
mobile. Most Lepidoptera are "beneficial":
• Pollinators: Adult Lepidoptera feed almost exclusively on
nectar. An army of pollinators at night.
• Herbivores: Larval Lepidoptera are largely feeding on
plants, some on fungi, hairs, feathers and feces. Breakdown
of dry leaf litter in Australia (Oecophoridae), nutrient
cycling.
• Pray: Larvae and adults are important parts of food webs.
https://doi.org/10.1111/een.12782
```

## Slide 4

[View slide 4](README.md#slide-4)

```text
Beneficial Lepidoptera
Cactoblastis cactorum (Pyralidae; "Cactus Moth")
• Native to South America.
• Feeds on Opuntia cactus species.
• Opuntia was introduced to Australia in 1788
(host for Dactylopius scale insects - used for red dyes).
• Opuntia made 24M ha land unusable.
• Introduction of Cactoblastis cactorum in 1926 had Opuntia
reduced by 50-80% in 1933 - biological weed control.
• Introduction to Africa and North America disasterous.
```

```text
Pest Lepidoptera
Tineola bisselliella (Tineidae; "Common Clothes Moth")
```

## Slide 6

[View slide 6](README.md#slide-6)

```text
Pest Lepidoptera
Bollworms, armyworms & cutworms (Noctuidae): Agricultural damage.
Helicoverpa armigera (Noctuidae; "Cotton Bollworm")
• Feeds on > 200 host plants.
• Causes > US$ 2 billion / year damage globally.
• Australian cotton industry most affected.
• Resistance to insecticides develops quickly
• Use of GM cotton "Bollgard 3" effective:
Three proteins that kill larvae in different ways.
• Occurs throughout Old World.
• Sister species Helicoverpa zea a New World pest.
• Introduction of H. armigera to New World resulted in hybridisation: New H.
armigera / zea mega-pest.
```

## Slide 8

[View slide 8](README.md#slide-8)

```text
Pest Lepidoptera
Lymantria dispar (Erebidae; "Gypsy Moth")
• Native to Europe and Asia, spread to North America.
• Population dynamics: From low density to outbreak cycles.
• Feeds on > 300 trees and shrubs, defoliating entire forests.
• Females lay batches of eggs on any substrate, including
shipping containers that arrive in Australia.
• First instar larvae spread via "ballooning": Long-haired
caterpillars attached to silken thread and blown by wind.
```

```text
Pest Lepidoptera
Epiphyas postvittana (Tortricidae; "Light Brown Apple Moth" - LBAM)
• Important pest of apple, citrus and grapes.
• Native to Australia, spread to New Zealand,
New Caledonia, UK and USA.
Phyllocnistis citrella (Gracillariidae; "Citrus Leafminer" - CLM)
• Important pest of various citrus species.
• Mines within leaves (minuscule).
• Believed to have originated from Asia and spread to Australia and New
World.
• Masters student revising the genus found cryptic sister species in
Australia (true origin of pest).
```

```text
Pest Lepidoptera
Ochrogaster lunifer (Notodontidae; "Bag Shelter Moth")
• Native to Australia.
• Larvae are gregarious
• Live "socially" inside a bag shelter.
• Follow each other head-to-tail ("Processionary Spinners")
when moving out to forage.
• Hairs of larvae are very urticating: Itchy, blindness.
```

```text
Peculiar: Australia's Scribbly Gum Moth
•
Distinctive markings on around 20 species of smooth-barked gum tree.
•
1930’s: Markings caused by feeding of the larvae of a very small moth.

Adult female lays eggs between old and new layers of bark.

Larva feeds on next year’s bark, and the scar is revealed when the bark is
shed.
```
