## Slide 4

[View slide 4](README.md#slide-4)

```text
Narrowing down & triaging
1. Identify to (super)family level.
• Difficulties:
• Morphology “hidden” beneath scales.
• Current family concepts should but do not always reflect
natural groups (monophyletic) => recent changes in
classification based on molecular data.
• Characters defining families are limited in number and not
always diagnostic / often difficult to see without dissection.
• Dichotomous keys are hard to follow and long (exceptions).
• Most practical key in The Families of Malesian Moths and
Butterflies (Holloway, Kibby & Peggie 2000).
• Lucid key to families with pests (Anderson, Luo & Bellis 2017).
https://keys.lucidcentral.org/search/lepidopteran-families-of-biosecurity-concern/
• “Cheat-sheet” with diagnostic family characters.
```

```text
SUMMARY
●Extremely species rich: 160k named species globally, 10k in Australia.
●Species-level identification difficult – ID to (super)family level with
morphology, further through similarity in appearance.
●Diagnostic structures are:
●Head scaling
●Chaetosemata
●Ocelli
●Proboscis scaling
●Morphology of antenna
●Labial and maxillary palpi
●Tympanal organs
●Wing shape and venation
●Four species-rich superfamilies:
●Gelechioidea, Pyraloidea, Geometroidea & Noctuoidea
```
